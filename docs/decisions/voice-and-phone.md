# Voice conversations and the phone

Built 2026-11-23: voice in the web app (the chat page and the chat beside a mailbox), then the phone: the foundation (settings, provider adapter, numbers), incoming calls and outgoing calls. Nothing here was run against a real telephony or speech provider: see "Verifying against real services" at the end.

## Problem

Zetta had a microphone for the message box (speech to text, the words land in the box) and a Listen button (text to speech, one message at a time). Neither is a conversation. A person who wants to talk with a bot, hands busy or away from the keyboard, had no way to do it, and a bot had no way to be reached by phone.

## Decision

The person talks with a bot **live inside Zetta**, and the bot answers in speech. The conversation is saved as **ordinary chat messages**. The answer stays **the bot's own**: its memory, its instructions, its tools and its permissions. A voice call is not a second, lighter bot.

Pipeline, for each thing the person says: speech to text for that utterance, then the bot's normal turn, then text to speech sentence by sentence, with barge-in (the person speaks over the bot and the bot stops).

- **One session, many doors.** `VoiceSession` (`modules/voice/core/session.ts`) knows nothing about a network. The voice activity detector, the recognizer, the synthesizer, the bot's turn and the clock are injected. The browser socket feeds it frames today; a phone line will feed it the same frames.
- **Transport.** A WebSocket carrying binary frames of 16 kHz mono 16-bit PCM, 20 ms (640 bytes) each, in both directions, plus small JSON control messages. A phone line's 8 kHz mu-law is converted at its edge (`mulawDecode`, `resample` in `core/audio.ts`), so the session never sees it.
- **The chat is the record.** What the person said is their message in that conversation, marked `meta.voice.callId`. The bot's reply is saved like any reply, with `meta.voice.spokenChars` (how much of it the person heard before a barge-in). Anyone who opens the chat later reads the call.
- **Same scope, same permissions.** A spoken message starts the turn through `chat.startTurn`, exactly as a typed one does. A message said while the bot works goes through the late-message path of fluid chat (`chat.enqueue`: the bot reads it at its next step, a running command is never cut). The words "stop", "para" and the rest of the chat's stop phrases call the chat's stop. Nothing in a call can do what a typed message cannot.
- **A voice note for the bot, not for the speaker.** The bot gets a short addendum on a spoken turn only (`modules/voice/prompt.ts`): answer in plain sentences, no markdown or code (put code in the chat text and say so), and say one short sentence before work that takes more than a few seconds. It is added to the prompt of the turn, not to the saved message. About 600 characters at most.

## Where a call can be held

Only a plain chat between the person and one bot. A room (`discussion`) and a chat with outside people (`inbound`) are refused when the ticket is asked for. Someone else's chat is "not found". Voice must be on: speech to text and text to speech both set up in Studio (`GET /api/voice/capabilities`).

## The socket

`POST /api/voice/ticket {conversationId}` is authenticated, origin-checked and limited to 12 a minute per person. It answers a one-time ticket that expires in about 30 seconds (`expiresInSeconds`). `GET /api/voice/socket?ticket=` is the WebSocket: a browser sends no custom header on a WebSocket, so the ticket is the credential (the same pattern as the VNC viewer of computers), and the Origin is checked again at the upgrade. Both routes are **worker-only** (`worker-only.ts`, `docker/scale/Caddyfile`): the ticket lives in the memory of the process that serves the socket, and the call runs the bot's turn, which only the worker can do (see `docs/operations/scaling.md`). `GET /api/voice/capabilities` can be served by any instance.

Messages from the page: `start {language?}`, `mute`, `unmute`, `end`, `played {samples}` (what the player has played of the current reply, so `spokenChars` is what was heard, not what was sent). From the server: `state {listening|thinking|speaking|ended, reason?}`, `transcript {text, final}`, `say {text}` (the browser's own voice: the page speaks this sentence), `clear` (flush playback now), `error {code, message}`.

The language of a call is, in order: the page's (`start.language`), the bot's `voice_language`, the installation's speech-to-text language.

## Barge-in, and the player

The server holds audio back so that no more than half a second waits at the player (`playback.ts`). Without that a sentence arrives in a moment, the session believes it has finished speaking, and a person talking over the still-playing audio is not recognised as a barge-in. A `clear` message empties the player. In the `browser` voice there is no audio to hold back, so the text synthesizer takes about as long as speaking the sentence would (65 ms a character) and the session stays in `speaking` while the device speaks. Known limit: in the last half second of a reply, after the session has gone back to `listening`, the person's words still cut the player (the server sends `clear` with the transcript), but the cut comes with the end of their sentence, not with its first word.

## Limits and what ends a call

One call per person at a time: a second one ends the first, with the reason `replaced` (socket close code 4409). A call ends on `end`, on the socket closing (`disconnected`), after 30 minutes (`time_limit`), or after 2 minutes without speech from either side (`idle`; a bot that is working or speaking is not silence). The server pings every 20 seconds, and the route's idle timeout is the largest Bun allows, so a muted, quiet call is not cut. Every end writes a `state ended` message with its reason first.

## Data

`zetta.voice_call`: the call itself (who, which bot, which chat, channel `web | phone`, direction `in | out`, `peer`, trust `owner | outside`, status `ringing | active | ended | failed | blocked`, end reason, language, times, duration, `owner_instance`). Deleting the chat deletes its calls. `bot.voice` and `bot.voice_language`: the bot's voice, on the server, because a phone call has no browser to remember it. They are part of a bot's saved versions. A call writes two audit events (kind `voice_call`: `started` and `ended:<reason>`; ids and a closed reason only, no words).

The Voice section of the bot settings keeps the browser's own voice on the device (a device voice has a name that means nothing on another device, and a phone call cannot use it) and saves a provider's voice and language on the bot. A voice chosen on a device before this was kept on the server is offered again once, and leaves the device when saved. `POST /api/speech` takes an optional `botId` and uses the bot's saved voice when no voice is sent.

## Instances and recovery

The live call is in the memory of one worker. Its row names that instance. Recovery follows the other work (`runtime/instance.ts`): at boot and on each tick the instance that runs work marks the open calls of a dead instance `failed` with the reason `restart`. A call of a live instance is never touched. The chat messages of a call that was cut stay; the bot's turn for the last utterance finishes in the chat like any other.

## The voice that runs in the server

A phone call cannot use the browser's voice, and OpenAI's `/audio/speech` models are listed by OpenAI as shut down on 2027-01-06, so the installation needs a voice that needs no provider. `speechProvider = local` is Kokoro-82M (Apache-2.0) through `@huggingface/transformers`, with eSpeak NG (WASM) as the phonemizer: `modules/speech/local.ts`. Reasons for Kokoro over Piper, and the measurements, are in `docs/operations/voice-and-documents.md` and `docs/verification/e2e/voice-quality.md`. It is a `Synthesizer` like the remote one (16 kHz chunks, one per sentence), so the session, the Listen button and a future phone line use it without a change. No database change: `speech_provider` is a plain text column and the list of values lives in `packages/core/src/speech.ts`. Same limit as local Whisper: not in the single-file compiled binary.

## Phone

Decided with the user: bots get real phone numbers; they receive calls and may call **any** number (no allow list for outgoing calls). The owner of a bot does no setup: the one-time work (an account with the telephony provider, funding, a public address) is done by whoever operates the installation, in Studio; where it was not done, phone is not offered. Defaults taken: **Telnyx first** (an individual can hold a Brazilian number with CPF, ID and proof of address, reviewed in up to 3 business days; Twilio asks for a company for Brazilian local numbers), behind a provider interface; **Twilio was added second, and the provider is chosen per number** (below); a number is bought **when the owner asks**, not for every bot (each costs money every month); a fixed opening sentence on every call saying an automated assistant speaks for the owner; no recording; an installation-wide do-not-call list. Operator steps: `docs/operations/phone.md`.

### Built (the foundation, 2026-11-23)

- **Settings** in `installation_settings` (`phone_*`): provider `off | telnyx`, the sealed key (connection provider `phone`, purpose `installation.phone-key`), the public key for webhook signatures, the public address, the provider's call application id, the request mode (`off | owner_request | operator_approval`), the countries, and limits (most numbers, minutes per call, minutes and calls per bot per day). Studio card "Phone numbers" (and the Chief's installation card); `POST /api/installation/phone/test`.
- **Provider interface** `modules/phone/provider.ts` with `telnyx.ts` and a fake Telnyx server and webhook signer in `fake.ts`. Webhooks are verified with Ed25519 over `<timestamp>|<raw body>`, fail closed, refuse events older or newer than 5 minutes and refuse an event id seen before (until the handler releases it for the provider's retry).
- **Numbers** `zetta.phone_number` (status `requested | pending_review | active | held | released | failed`, inbound `anyone | owner_only | off`, outbound `on | off`, the owner's own number and a scrypt PIN hash with a lockout) and `zetta.phone_optout`; `voice_call.number_id` now references `phone_number` (set null). Routes under `/api/bots/:id/phone` and `/api/phone/*`; "Give this bot a phone number" in the bot settings; the Chief's `zetta:phone` block (cards; no PIN action). A background job syncs orders in review and releases numbers held past 30 days.
- `GET /api/voice/capabilities` answers `phone: on` only when a provider, its public key, a public address, a call application and a readable key are all set.

Decisions taken in this step that differ from a literal reading of the brief: a status `requested` (a request that waits for an administrator, no number yet) was added to the statuses; the E.164 number is unique only while the number is not released (a released number can be bought again); a bot has at most one number or request (`requested`, `pending_review`, `active`); a held number is not brought back when its bot is restored.

### Built: incoming calls (2026-11-23)

- **Door.** `POST /api/phone/webhook` (`call-routes.ts`, `calls.ts`): the signature, the age and the id of the event are checked first and fail closed; ids are recorded in `zetta.phone_webhook_event` so every instance sharing the database agrees (a replay is answered 409, a bad or stale event 401, never details). Events of one call are handled one at a time. `GET /api/phone/stream?token=` is the media WebSocket; the token is minted when the call is answered, travels in the `stream_url` of the answer action, works once and expires after 60 seconds. Both are worker-only (`worker-only.ts`, Caddyfile).
- **Audio.** Telnyx is asked for PCMU 8 kHz (changed 2026-11-24: its docs list L16 16 kHz but never state the byte order, so a first real call could have been noise; the code still reads big-endian L16 if a stream says it is L16); PCMU 8 kHz is converted with `mulawDecode` and `resample`. Chunks are put back in order (`createReorder`); the bot's audio goes out as `media` messages and a barge-in sends `clear`. Key presses come only from the webhook (`call.dtmf.received`), never from the stream, so a digit is not counted twice. The call runs the same `VoiceSession` as the web, with a phone transport (`calls.ts`).
- **Who is calling.** `plan.ts` decides from facts: a number that is not `active`, or inbound `off`, answers "not in service"; a bot already on a call says it is busy; `owner_only` turns away everyone except the owner's number with a PIN that can still be tried; the owner's number plus a PIN set means "ask for the PIN"; everyone else is outside. Every call opens with the fixed sentence (`words.ts`, `pt`/`es`/`en` by the bot's voice language), also the refused ones. Refused calls are answered (they need the stream to be spoken to), speak one fixed sentence and hang up; a number that already had 30 refusals in the last hour is rejected unanswered.
- **Owner call.** Right number and right PIN typed on the keypad (`#` sends, `*` clears, three wrong tries lock the number's PIN as `pin.ts` does, 15 s to start typing): a new ordinary `chat` conversation of the owner with the bot, `voice_call.trust = owner`. The turn is started with `voice.channel = "phone"`, which sets **`startedBy = "server"`** and so **`pluginTurn = "server"`**: the person did not type it, speech recognition errors and a faked caller ID make "apply at once" wrong, so what the bot writes becomes an Approve card (approval-policy.ts). The PIN is held in memory only for the entry.
- **Outside call.** An `inbound` conversation (`external.plugin = "phone"`, the caller's number as the chat key, one conversation per call), untrusted history, no code, no memory, no mail, no outgoing calls. What they say is wrapped as untrusted data. `inboundAllowed` lets a phone turn use **only** the `phone` plugin's `take_message(text)` (a system note in the conversation plus a `bot_ask` card for the owner, one live card per call) and `end_call(outcome)` (hangs up after the bot has finished speaking; the outcome `done | message_taken | wrong_number | spam | refused` goes to the audit trail). Not even a hand-off. Words said while the bot answers are held and run as the next turn of the same call (never queued, since a queued message would later run as the owner's own typing).
- **Do not call.** A small matcher in three languages (`asksNotToBeCalled`) puts the caller's number on `phone_optout` with reason `caller_request`, notes it in the conversation and in audit.
- **Limits.** One call at a time per bot (decided from the database, so another instance agrees); 5 minutes for an outside caller; the installation's longest call for the owner; per-bot calls and minutes of the last 24 hours (0 means none); 20 seconds without speech from either side ends the call (a bot that is working is not silence); a time limit says one fixed sentence first. The owner's number is not stopped by the day's quota at the start (an attacker could use it up); a caller who then fails the PIN is checked against it.
- **Records.** `voice_call` has one row per call (`channel phone`, `direction in`, `peer`, `trust`, `number_id`, `end_reason`, duration, language). `conversation_id` became nullable: a turned-away call has no conversation (migration `phone_calls`, with the table of seen webhook ids). Started and ended calls are in the audit trail as web calls are. The transcript is the conversation's ordinary messages, shown where inbound conversations show.
- Phone is offered only with a voice the server makes (`local` or a provider); with the browser voice only, `GET /api/voice/capabilities` says `phone: off` and a call is rejected unanswered.

### Built: outgoing calls (2026-11-23)

A bot with an `active` number whose outbound is `on` may place a call with the tool `phone_call({to, briefing, max_minutes?, voicemail?})` (plugin `phone_out`, `effect "send"`). The user's decisions: bots may call **any** number (outside people or the owner), with no allow list; the person's own request is the approval, exactly as for sending mail (`modules/chat/mail-policy.ts`); a call can never be remembered as "always allow".

**The decision** is pure and server side (`modules/chat/phone-policy.ts`), made on every call from facts the server gathered (`modules/phone/outbound-facts.ts`); nothing is a rule in a prompt.

| Turn                                                                                                                                                                                | Result                                                                                                                            |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| The person's own message in their own chat, they asked for a call, the number is one they wrote in it or the owner's registered number, and the turn read no outside content before | The call goes out at once.                                                                                                        |
| Any other allowed case: the person did not ask, the number came from a page, a mail or a tool result, the turn read outside content, a loop step, a routine, a turn Zetta started   | A card: the number, the country, the briefing, the longest it may take, what happens on a machine. One Approve. Never remembered. |
| A room, an inbound chat, a turn started by an outside event, a phone call's own turn                                                                                                | No tool at all (not offered, and refused if forced).                                                                              |

"The person asked" is `asksToCall` on the person's own words only (English, Portuguese, Spanish). Questions, drafts and "don't" forms do not count; when in doubt the answer is a card. A call to the owner's own registered number needs only the request ("call me").

**Hard blocks** (in code, whoever asked, also when a card is approved later): emergency numbers (per country, BR 100 180 188 190 191 192 193 194 197 198 199, US/CA 911 988, GB 999 112, EU 112 and others; also with a country code in front); anything that is not a valid E.164 number (`libphonenumber-js` `isValid`); another bot's number of this installation (a call loop); numbers on `phone_optout`; premium-rate and shared-cost numbers (`getType`); countries the operator did not allow (the installation's countries list, empty means none); the operator's daily calls and minutes per bot. The longest call is the smaller of what was asked, the operator's per-call cap and what is left of the day.

**Calling hours**: 08:00 to 20:00 at the destination (every time zone of a country that has several; Brazil by area code; a country the table does not know cannot be checked and counts as closed). They apply to a call the person did not ask for in that turn and refuse it outright (a card at night would only move the decision to a time nobody watches). They never apply to a call the person asked for now or to the owner's own number, and they are not checked again when a person approves a card (the person decided now).

**The call.** `modules/phone/calls.ts` `place` reads the blocks and limits again from fresh facts under the number's lock, inserts the `voice_call` row (`direction out`, `peer`, `trust outside`, `number_id`, `requested_in`), and dials through the provider with the stream to the existing media socket, answering machine detection, a time limit and the call id as `client_state`. The call then runs through the same `VoiceSession` as an incoming one:

1. Machine detection first (up to 8 s): a machine is hung up on, saying nothing, unless the person's card said `voicemail: leave_message`.
2. The **fixed opening** in the bot's language: "Hello, I'm an automated assistant calling on behalf of _owner name_." (pt, es too). Not removable.
3. The turn is **isolated**: a new `inbound` conversation for this call holding only the briefing the person approved; the bot's memory and the person's skills are left out (`withoutItems`); the other party's speech is untrusted outside content; the only tool is `phone_line.end_call(outcome, summary)` with `outcome` one of `done | declined | voicemail | failed` (the server sets `no_answer`). Every other block (memory, mail, a second call, messages, browser, hand-off) is refused by `inboundAllowed`.
4. 20 s without speech ends it, the time limit ends it, the person's **Hang up** ends it, and a callee who says they do not want calls ("don't call me", "não me ligue", "no me llames") is added to `phone_optout` (`caller_request`), told so, and the call ends.
5. No recording.

**What comes back.** A short system message in the chat that asked: outcome, length and two lines of summary (written by the call's bot from what the other party said, so shown as retold text and never followed), with a link to the call's own chat where the whole exchange is. `voice_call.outcome` holds the outcome.

**Records.** Migration `phone_call_outcome` (additive): `voice_call.outcome` (`done | declined | no_answer | voicemail | failed`) and `voice_call.requested_in` (the asking chat, set null when it is deleted). Every call, card, refusal and block is in the audit trail with the bot, the destination country and the outcome as closed tokens (for example `out:refused:premium_rate:us`); never the number, the briefing or a word that was said.

**Parity.** See `bot-parity.md`: bots have `phone_call`; the Chief reads call history in `zetta:phone` (`calls`) and cannot place a call; MCP has `list_calls` and `get_call` (read) and no call tool, because MCP has no approval card.

Not built: a spoken "press 1" menu, transfer to a person, an estimate of the cost per minute on the card (the settings hold no rate), a call that survives a server restart (it is marked failed like any open call), and a first sentence about the purpose before the other party speaks (the call waits for them after the fixed opening).

### Built: a number you already own (2026-11-23)

The administrator attaches a number the Telnyx account already holds and gives it to any bot that is not archived and has no number (Zetta included), or moves a number to another bot. No order, no review, no migration: the row is inserted `active` with the bot's owner and the provider's id. Decisions: the provider is asked (`findNumber`) before anything is saved and the number is routed to the call application (`routeNumber`, `PATCH /phone_numbers/{id}` with `connection_id`); only administrators do it; the Chief does it as an approval card each time and it is never remembered; the settings are never environment variables (`phone/no-env.test.ts`). Reassigning clears the registered own number, the PIN and the hold because they belonged to the old owner, and is refused during a live call. The picker (`GET /api/phone/bots`) shows other people's bots to administrators with the owner's display name, not the e-mail. Not run against a real Telnyx account (details in `docs/operations/phone.md`).

### Built: Twilio as a second provider (2026-11-23)

The user decided: **the provider is chosen per number, Twilio or Telnyx, and both can be set up at the same time**, each with its own sealed key. One installation may hold a Brazilian number at Telnyx and a US number at Twilio. Nothing is read from environment variables (a test enforces it for the module).

- **Settings, additive only** (migration `phone_twilio`, nothing renamed, no data touched): `phone_twilio` (`off | on`), `phone_twilio_account_sid`, `phone_twilio_connection_id` (the sealed Auth Token, connection provider `phonetwilio`, purpose `installation.phone-twilio-key`) and `phone_default_provider` (`telnyx | twilio`, default `telnyx`). The older names are kept and now read as the **Telnyx** part: `phone_provider` (`off | telnyx`) is the Telnyx switch, `phone_connection_id` its key, `phone_public_key` and `phone_application_id` are Telnyx's; `phone_public_url`, the request mode, the countries and the limits are shared. `phone_number.provider` accepts `twilio` (its CHECK was widened; it already existed on every number). The alternative of renaming `phone_provider` to something neutral was rejected: a rename of a column in use buys nothing and risks every deployed row.
- **The adapter is chosen by the number.** The reader gives `provider(id)` and `providerFor(row)`; an incoming call finds its number first and then the adapter (a number reached through the other provider's door is turned away); outgoing calls, release, sync and hold use the number's own provider. `offer()` lists the ready providers, the countries (one list) and the default. A request or an attach takes an optional `provider`; without it the default is used when ready, else the only ready one; a named provider that is not ready is refused with a sentence that names it. In approval mode the request keeps its provider.
- **`calls.ts` stays provider-neutral** through a small `caps` object on each provider: where keys come from (`dtmf`: the webhook for Telnyx, the media stream for Twilio), what says a call was answered (`answered`), the stream codec (`L16` for Telnyx, `PCMU` for Twilio, whose stream is 8 kHz mu-law only), where the one-time stream token travels (`streamToken`: the address's query, or a custom parameter because Twilio's stream address takes no query), and whether messages on the socket carry the `streamSid`. No place in `calls.ts` tests a provider's name.
- **Twilio answers a call in the HTTP response to its webhook (TwiML)**, not with a later command. The Twilio adapter turns each webhook into the same events the state machine already knows (`call.initiated`, `call.answered`, `call.machine.detection.ended`, `call.hangup`); `answer` and `reject` fill a **pending answer** kept per call, which the voice route is still waiting for and reads after the event was handled (`reply`); with no answer in 8 seconds the call is rejected (`<Reject reason="busy"/>`). A call answered with `<Connect><Stream url="wss://…/api/phone/stream"><Parameter name="token" value="…"/></Stream></Connect><Hangup/>`. `hangup` completes the call through the REST API. An outgoing call is created with that TwiML inline and asks for asynchronous answering machine detection.
- **Routes**: `POST /api/phone/twilio/voice` (a number rings, answered with TwiML), `POST /api/phone/twilio/status` (call status and machine detection), the existing `GET /api/phone/stream` (the token now also arrives in the stream's `start` message; a socket that names no call in 15 seconds is closed). All worker-only, in `worker-only.ts` and the Caddyfile.
- **Webhook verification fails closed**: the signature (HMAC-SHA1 with the Auth Token over the configured public address plus the path and the sorted form parameters, constant-time compare), the host of the request (a request naming another host than the configured public address is refused), the Account SID of the call, and the replay: Twilio's signature has no timestamp, so each event is claimed by `CallSid` + status + sequence number (or `CallSid` + machine result, or `CallSid` for the voice webhook) in the same database table as Telnyx's ids.
- **Numbers at Twilio**: search is local and voice-enabled; a purchase sets the voice and status addresses of the number in the same request. A country whose numbers need papers (Twilio's `address_requirements` is anything but `none`, as Brazil's are, companies only) is **not bought**: the answer says to buy the number in the Twilio console with the company's bundle and attach it. Accepting a bundle and address as optional settings was not built: Twilio's pages say a bundle and an address may be required, but not how they are chosen per number or per country.
- **Cost**: Twilio's search returns no price, so none is shown.
- **Tests**: `twilio.test.ts` (adapter against `fake-twilio.ts`), `twilio.integration.test.ts` (full calls through the real routes and a real socket, both directions, with a fake Telnyx beside it), `providers.integration.test.ts` (two providers at once through the real reader), `phone-twilio-settings.test.ts`, `wire.test.ts`, `phone.test.ts` in `packages/core`, and the migration test `phone-twilio.migration.test.ts`. **No real Twilio account or call was used**; what is unproven is listed in `docs/operations/phone.md`.

## Not decided here

A model-based voice activity detector (the energy detector hears any loud sound and cannot tell the bot's own voice coming back through a speaker, so a page must use the browser's echo cancellation). Streaming speech to text before the person has finished (today each utterance is recognised when the pause ends, started speculatively at the first pause). Both sit behind interfaces the session already has.

## Verifying against real services

What was run, and what was not. Run against real things: the real Whisper model through the real socket, the web page in a real browser (Helium) with a fake microphone and the browser's own voice, and the Kokoro voice on this server (`docs/verification/e2e/voice-call.md`, `voice-page.md`, `voice-quality.md`). Not run against anything real: any telephony provider, any paid speech provider, a real microphone with a room and echo, and a voice call whose bot answers with a real model (the e2e bot engine was a script).
